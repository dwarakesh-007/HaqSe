# Right to Information (RTI)

## What RTI is
Any Indian citizen can ask a government office for information, and they must reply. You do not have to say why you want it.

Examples:
- "What is the status of my ration card / pension / scholarship application?"
- "How much money was spent on the road in my area?"
- "Copies of the file notings on my complaint"

## What you cannot get
Some things are exempt, like matters of national security, someone else's private details, or cabinet papers. The office must give a reason if they refuse.

## Step-by-step path
1. **Find the right office** and address the application to its Public Information Officer (PIO).
2. **Write the application.** Keep the questions short and specific. The app drafts this for you.
3. **Pay the fee.** Rs 10 for central government offices. State fees and payment methods differ (see the state file). People below the poverty line (BPL) don't pay, with proof.
4. **Submit it.**
   - Central government offices: online at rtionline.gov.in
   - State offices: by post or in person, or the state's online portal if it has one
5. **Wait for the reply.** The office must reply within **30 days** (48 hours if it concerns someone's life or liberty).

## If you get no reply or a bad reply
1. **First appeal** to the First Appellate Authority in the same office, within **30 days**.
2. **Second appeal** to the Information Commission (Central or State), within **90 days** of the first appeal decision.

## Evidence to keep
- A copy of the application
- Postal receipt or online acknowledgement
- Fee payment proof
- Any reply received

## What happens at the Information Commission (walkthrough)
RTI does not go to a regular court. The last step is the Information Commission.
1. **File the second appeal** with the State (or Central) Information Commission, with copies of your application, any reply, and the first appeal.
2. **Hearing:** the commission calls you and the Public Information Officer. Many commissions allow phone or video hearings. No lawyer needed.
3. **Decision:** the commission can order the office to give you the information.
4. **Penalty:** if the officer delayed or refused without good reason, the commission can fine the officer (up to Rs 25,000) and you may get compensation.

## Case strength check
Strong signs:
- Your questions were clear and asked for records, not opinions
- You have proof of filing and fee payment
- The 30-day deadline passed with no reply, or the reply was clearly incomplete
- You filed the first appeal on time

Weak signs (and how to fix them):
- Questions asked "why" or for opinions → re-file asking for documents and records
- Asked for someone else's private details → likely to be refused
- Missed the appeal deadline → explain the reason for the delay in the appeal
