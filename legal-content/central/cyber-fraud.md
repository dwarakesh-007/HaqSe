# Cyber fraud and online scams

## What counts as cyber fraud
- UPI or bank fraud (money taken without your permission)
- Fake calls pretending to be bank, police, courier or customs ("digital arrest" scams)
- Fake job offers or part-time task scams
- Fake investment or trading apps
- Someone using your photos or making a fake profile
- Your account being hacked
- Online shopping where you paid but never got anything

## The most important thing: act fast
The first few hours matter most. The faster you report, the better the chance of freezing the money.

## Step-by-step path
1. **Call 1930 immediately.** This is the national cyber fraud helpline. They can try to freeze the money in the fraudster's account.
2. **Report online at cybercrime.gov.in** (National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal). Save the acknowledgement number.
3. **Tell your bank right away.** Block your card or UPI, and ask them to raise a dispute. Get it in writing.
4. **Go to your nearest cyber crime police station** if the portal asks, or if the amount is large. Carry your evidence.
5. **Change your passwords** and log out of all devices if your account was hacked.

## Important to tell the user
- Police, CBI, customs or courts **never** do "digital arrest" over video calls. This is always a scam.
- No real bank or officer will ask for OTP, PIN or to install a screen-sharing app.
- If money was taken because of a bank's system problem and you report quickly, RBI rules may protect you from losing it. Report within 3 working days. (Check current RBI rules before launch.)

## Evidence to keep
- Screenshots of the chat, call log, SMS, website or app
- Transaction ID, UTR number, date and time
- The fraudster's phone number, UPI ID or account number
- Bank statement showing the deduction

## When this app should say "get human help"
- The user is being threatened or blackmailed
- Sextortion or leaked private photos (point to cybercrime.gov.in "women/child related crime" section and police)
- The user is accused of a crime
Point the user to the police and to the District Legal Services Authority (DLSA) for free legal aid.

## What happens after you report (court walkthrough)
Cyber fraud is a crime, so the case is run by the police and the government, not by you. You are the complainant and a witness.
1. **Report:** 1930 and cybercrime.gov.in. The money may be frozen in the receiving accounts.
2. **FIR:** the police register a case. Ask for a copy of the FIR; it is your right and it is free.
3. **Investigation:** police trace the accounts and phone numbers. This can take weeks or months.
4. **Getting frozen money back:** the court (usually the magistrate) can order the bank to release frozen money to you. The police or a free legal aid lawyer can help you apply.
5. **Trial:** if the fraudster is caught, the police file a chargesheet and the case goes to trial. You may be called to give your statement.

You do not need a private lawyer for most of this. Free legal aid (DLSA) can help with the release application.

## Case strength check
Strong signs:
- Reported to 1930 within a few hours
- Have the transaction ID / UTR, date and time
- Have screenshots of chats, calls, SMS or the fake app
- Bank informed in writing quickly

Weak signs (and how to fix them):
- Reported very late → report anyway; late reports can still be traced
- Paid in cash or crypto → harder to trace; still report it
- Shared OTP or PIN yourself → recovery from the bank is harder, but the fraudster still committed a crime; report it
- Deleted the chats → check the other device, cloud backup, or bank SMS
